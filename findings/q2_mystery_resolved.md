# Q2 mystery, resolved: the two numbers were never measuring the same thing

## The puzzle
Our flow accounting (Figure 2) showed only a **-1.1 mb/d** net gap after Hormuz's loss (-6.1 mb/d) is offset by Petroline's gain (+5.0 mb/d). But the IEA separately reports **12.8-14 mb/d "shut in"** and **"exceeds 1 billion barrels"** of cumulative losses since February — a much bigger number.

## The resolution
These are two different kinds of measurement, not two conflicting answers to the same question:

- **Our -1.1 mb/d figure is a *transit* metric** — how much oil physically moved through one chokepoint (the Strait) plus its one main bypass pipeline (Petroline).
- **The IEA's 12.8-14 mb/d figure is a *production* metric** — how much oil the whole Gulf region pumped out of the ground, compared to before the war.

Those aren't the same pool of barrels:
- **Not all Gulf oil goes through Hormuz in the first place.** Iraq ships a lot of its crude out through a pipeline to the Mediterranean (Kirkuk-Ceyhan) that has nothing to do with the Strait. Saudi Arabia already sends some oil out via the Red Sea in normal times too. So a chunk of the "shut-in" production was never going to show up in a Hormuz transit count either way — comparing the two is like comparing a city's train ridership to the whole region's population.
- **The arithmetic checks out internally.** ~14 mb/d held back for about 70 days (late Feb to mid-April) works out to roughly 980 million barrels — which matches the IEA's own "exceeds 1 billion barrels" claim. So the shut-in number isn't a typo or an exaggeration, it's just answering a different question than "how much less oil went through the Strait."

Confirmed directly from the IEA's own wording: *"cumulative supply losses from Gulf producers already exceed 1 billion barrels with more than 14 mb/d of oil now shut in"* — this is production language ("shut in" = wells/fields not producing), not transit language.

## Bottom line
Vahan's instinct that "something besides rerouting was going on" was right, but not for the reason he guessed (wells shutting down because inventories were low). The real reason our two numbers looked contradictory is simpler: one chart measured a chokepoint, the other measured a whole region's oil output. Different questions, so it's not surprising they gave very different-looking numbers.
